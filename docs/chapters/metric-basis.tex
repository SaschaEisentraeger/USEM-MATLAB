\chapter{Basis of the metric Vandermonde matrix}
\label{ch:metric-basis}

% Line colours of the plots in this chapter (monomial, Legendre, Chebyshev,
% Lagrange, ...)
\pgfplotscreateplotcyclelist{metricbasis}{%

The metric (trial) shape functions are obtained from a generalized Vandermonde
matrix (\cref{ch:theory}). The original implementation used monomials
$s^a t^b$ in skew coordinates. This chapter examines whether a Vandermonde
matrix built from other polynomials is advantageous, explains why the Lagrange
basis of the reference element was chosen as the new default, and documents a
limitation that no choice of basis can remove. The numbers are produced by
\code{studies/metric_basis/study_metric_basis.m} and
\code{studies/metric_basis/probe_hybrid_space.m} (MATLAB or GNU Octave); the
tests are in \code{tests/unit/MetricBasisTest.m}. The data in
\code{docs/figures/data/metric-basis-*.dat} were computed with GNU Octave~8.4
(IEEE double precision, LAPACK LU); values at round-off level can differ in the
last digits in MATLAB.

\section{Setting and basis invariance}

Let $\{\vect x_k\}_{k=1}^{n}$, $n=(p_\xi+1)(p_\eta+1)$, be the element nodes,
$(s_k,t_k)$ their skew coordinates, and $\{\phi_c\}_{c=1}^{n}$ a basis of
$Q_p(s,t)$. With $V_{ck}=\phi_c(\vect x_k)$ and $P_{cq}=\phi_c(\vect x_q)$ at the
query points, the code computes $\matr N=\matr V^{-1}\matr P$,
$N_{lq}=M_l(\vect x_q)$.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (basis invariance).} If $\matr V$ is non-singular, the
metric shape functions are the unique functions $M_l\in Q_p(s,t)$ with
$M_l(\vect x_k)=\delta_{lk}$; they do not depend on the basis.

\emph{Proof.} For another basis $\bm\psi=\matr C\bm\phi$ with $\matr C$
non-singular, $\matr V_\psi=\matr C\matr V_\phi$ and $\matr P_\psi=\matr
C\matr P_\phi$, hence $\matr V_\psi^{-1}\matr P_\psi=\matr V_\phi^{-1}\matr
P_\phi$. \hfill$\square$

Consequently, completeness, frame invariance and distortion immunity
(\cref{ch:theory}) are unaffected by the basis, and so is unisolvence
(singularity of $\matr V$ is a property of the node set and the space). The basis
only changes the floating-point error. For LU with partial pivoting the computed
solution of $\matr V\matr N=\matr P$ satisfies the classical bound
$\|\hat{\matr N}-\matr N\|/\|\matr N\|\lesssim c_n\,\kappa(\matr V)\,u$ with unit
round-off $u\approx1.1\times10^{-16}$ \parencite{Higham2002Accuracy}, so
$\kappa(\matr V)$ is the quantity to minimize.

\section{Candidate bases}

\code{PolynomialBasis1D(s, n, basis, ndist)} provides, with values and first
derivatives,
\begin{center}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lL@{}}
    \toprule
    \code{basis} & $\phi_a(s)$, $a=0,\dots,n$ \\
    \midrule
    \code{'monomial'} & $s^a$ (original implementation) \\
    \code{'legendre'} & Legendre polynomials $P_a(s)$, three-term recurrence \\
    \code{'chebyshev'} & Chebyshev polynomials $T_a(s)$, three-term recurrence \\
    \code{'lagrange'} & Lagrange polynomials $\ell_a(s)$ through the 1D reference
      nodes \code{nodal_distribution(n, ndist)}, product formula (no division by
      $s-s_m$, so query points may coincide with nodes) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The 2D basis is the tensor product $\phi_a(\tilde s)\phi_b(\tilde t)$. The
Legendre, Chebyshev and Lagrange bases are defined on $[-1,1]$, but on distorted
elements the skew coordinates of the nodes leave $[-1,1]^2$ (up to $|s|=1.27$ for
the trapezoid of \code{usemTestElement}), where these polynomials grow like
$|T_p(s)|=\cosh(p\,\operatorname{arcosh}|s|)$; $T_{16}(1.27)\approx4\times10^4$.
For these bases \code{ShapeFunctions2D_Metric} therefore maps the bounding box of
the nodes in $(s,t)$ affinely onto $[-1,1]^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde s = (s-c_s)/r_s,\qquad \tilde t=(t-c_t)/r_t ,
\end{equation}
with centre $c$ and half width $r$ of the box. The map is affine and computed from
frame-invariant quantities, so $Q_p$ and frame invariance are preserved, and it is
the identity for parallelograms. The monomial branch is left unscaled, so
\code{p.metricBasis = 'monomial'} reproduces the previous implementation.

\section{Analysis}

\paragraph{Affine elements.} For a parallelogram $(s_k,t_k)$ are the reference
nodes, which form a tensor grid, so $\matr V$ is a column permutation of
$\matr V_t\otimes\matr V_s$ (1D Vandermonde matrices) and
$\kappa_2(\matr V)=\kappa_2(\matr V_s)\,\kappa_2(\matr V_t)$. For monomials on
real nodes symmetric about the origin, $\kappa(\matr V_s)$ grows at least
exponentially \parencite{Gautschi1988Lower}; the 2D condition number is the
square of the 1D one. For the Lagrange basis on the same reference nodes
$\phi_c(\vect x_k)=\delta_{ck}$ up to the ordering, i.e.\ $\matr V$ is a
permutation matrix, $\kappa(\matr V)=1$, and $\matr N=\matr V^{-1}\matr P$ is
obtained without amplification of round-off: metric and parametric functions
agree to round-off and $\matr K_e$ is symmetric to round-off for every $p$.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (distorted elements, Lagrange basis).} Let
$\hat{\vect s}_d$ be the reference nodes in the scaled skew coordinates, so that
$\phi_c(\hat{\vect s}_d)=\delta_{cd}$, and let
$\lambda_M(\vect s)=\sum_l|M_l(\vect s)|$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}(\vect s)=\sum_c|\phi_c(\vect s)|$ be the Lebesgue
functions of the metric and of the reference interpolation. Then
\begin{equation}
  (\matr V^{-1})_{ld}=M_l(\hat{\vect s}_d),\qquad
  \kappa_1(\matr V)=\max_k\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}(\tilde{\vect s}_k)\;
                    \max_d\lambda_M(\hat{\vect s}_d).
  \label{eq:kappa-lagrange}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} Evaluating $\matr N=\matr V^{-1}\matr P$ at the query points
$\hat{\vect s}_d$ gives $\matr P=\matr I$, hence the first identity. The 1-norm is
the maximum column sum: $\|\matr V\|_1=\max_k\sum_c|\phi_c(\tilde{\vect s}_k)|$ and
$\|\matr V^{-1}\|_1=\max_d\sum_l|M_l(\hat{\vect s}_d)|$. \hfill$\square$

With the bounding-box scaling all $\tilde{\vect s}_k\in[-1,1]^2$, so the first
factor is bounded by the Lebesgue constant of the reference tensor grid
($\le 6.1$ for GLL nodes and $p\le16$), and $\kappa_1(\matr V)$ is essentially
the Lebesgue function of the metric interpolation at the reference nodes. The
Lebesgue constant $\Lambda_M=\max_{\vect x\in\Omega_e}\lambda_M(\vect x)$ is a
property of the space and the node set, not of the basis: it is the factor by
which the metric interpolant amplifies perturbations of nodal values (e.g.\ the
round-off of a computed displacement field), and it enters the interpolation
error, $\|u-\mathcal I_M u\|_\infty\le(1+\Lambda_M)\inf_{q\in Q_p}\|u-q\|_\infty$.
\Cref{eq:kappa-lagrange} therefore states that the Lagrange basis adds no
ill-conditioning beyond that inherent in the interpolation problem (up to the
factor $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the difference between $\lambda_M$ at the
reference nodes and on the element). Orthogonal bases have no such identity but
behave similarly in practice (\cref{sec:metric-basis-results}); for them
$\kappa(\matr V)>1$ also on affine elements.

\section{Numerical results}
\label{sec:metric-basis-results}

\paragraph{Test cases.} The four elements of \code{usemTestElement}
(parallelogram; trapezoid with straight edges; 8-node element with two curved
edges; angular and curved-edge distortion combined), GLL nodes, $p=1,\dots,16$.
Measured quantities (\code{study_metric_basis.m}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\kappa_2(\matr V)$;
  \item $e_\delta=\max_{l,k}|M_l(\vect x_k)-\delta_{lk}|$, the error of the
    computed shape functions for worst-case (unit) nodal data;
  \item $e_P$, $e_{\nabla P}$: errors of the reproduction of all monomials of
    total degree $\le p$ in normalized Cartesian coordinates and of their scaled
    gradients at the $(2p)^2$ Gauss points of \code{Ke_2D};
  \item $\Lambda_M$, $\Lambda_N$: Lebesgue constants of the metric and of the
    parametric interpolation on a $(4p+9)^2$ grid in the element;
  \item $\|\matr K_e-\matr K_e^{\T}\|_F/\|\matr K_e\|_F$ on the parallelogram;
  \item at mesh level ($3\times2$ mesh, angular and curved-edge distortion $0.3$,
    \code{tests/integration/PatchTest.m}): the harmonic patch test of degree $p$
    and the field $\vect u=(e^x\cos y,\,-e^x\sin y)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Conditioning (\cref{fig:metric-basis-kappa}).} With monomials
$\kappa_2(\matr V)$ grows exponentially already on the parallelogram,
from $2.9\times10^2$ ($p=4$) over $2.9\times10^5$ ($p=8$) to
$3.5\times10^{11}$ ($p=16$), a factor of about $5.7$ per degree, close to
$(1+\sqrt2)^2\approx5.8$, i.e.\ about $(1+\sqrt2)^{p}$ for each of the two 1D
factors. On the parallelogram the Lagrange basis gives $\kappa_2=1$ for every
$p$, Chebyshev $\approx2.6$, Legendre grows linearly ($55$ at $p=16$). On the
distorted elements all three alternatives are within a factor of $3$ of one
another; their gain over the monomials is a factor $6$--$13$ at $p=4$ and
$1$--$5\times10^4$ at $p=16$ (trapezoid
$5.0\times10^{15}\to3.8$--$8.6\times10^{11}$, curved
$1.4\times10^{13}\to3.1$--$4.8\times10^{8}$, general
$9.0\times10^{14}\to0.9$--$2.0\times10^{11}$). Without the bounding-box scaling
the alternatives lose more than three orders of magnitude on the trapezoid
($\kappa_2=2.2\times10^{15}$ instead of $8.6\times10^{11}$ for the Lagrange basis
at $p=16$).

\paragraph{Accuracy of the shape functions (\cref{fig:metric-basis-kron}).}
$e_\delta$ follows $\kappa_2(\matr V)\,u$. On the parallelogram the monomials give
$e_\delta=2.0\times10^{-12}$ ($p=8$), $2.4\times10^{-9}$ ($p=12$) and
$1.1\times10^{-6}$ ($p=16$); with the Lagrange basis $e_\delta\le10^{-28}$. On
the distorted elements at $p=16$ the monomials give $3.0\times10^{-2}$
(trapezoid), $4.9\times10^{-5}$ (curved), $6.5\times10^{-4}$ (general), the
Lagrange basis $7.1\times10^{-6}$, $6.0\times10^{-9}$, $1.8\times10^{-6}$; the
Legendre basis is equally good. For $p\le6$ all bases give
$e_\delta<5\times10^{-13}$.

\paragraph{Polynomial reproduction (\cref{fig:metric-basis-leb}, right).} The
reproduction errors $e_P$ and $e_{\nabla P}$ are practically the same for all
bases, also for the monomials, and follow $u\Lambda_M$ (trapezoid, $p=16$:
$e_P\approx2\times10^{-9}$, $e_{\nabla P}\approx1$--$3\times10^{-8}$). The reason:
for nodal data $\vect f=\matr V^{\T}\vect c$ of a function in the space, the
first-order error of the computed interpolant is
$\vect c^{\T}\delta\matr V\,\matr N$ with the backward error $\delta\matr V$ of the
LU factorization, $|\vect c^{\T}\delta\matr V\matr N|\lesssim
u\,\|\vect c\|\,\|\matr V\|\,\Lambda_M$, and $\|\vect c\|\|\matr V\|$ is moderate
for low-degree polynomials in every basis. For generic nodal data (e.g.\ unit
vectors, as in $e_\delta$, or round-off-contaminated or oscillatory fields)
$\|\vect c\|$ can be as large as $\|\matr V^{-1}\|\,\|\vect f\|$, and the error
is $\mathcal O(\kappa(\matr V)\,u)$. The element matrices are built from the
individual functions $M_l$ and $\nabla M_l$, not from reproduced polynomials,
which is why the asymmetry of $\matr K_e$ on the parallelogram follows $e_\delta$:

(maximum relative nodal error; Legendre and Chebyshev lie in the same ranges,

rapidly decaying coefficients, so, as explained above, they are insensitive to

range $p\le12$ used so far; the gain of the new basis lies in the accuracy of the
individual shape functions and element matrices (symmetry on affine elements,
Kronecker property used by the GLL nodal-quadrature mass matrix, generic nodal
data such as eigenvectors or wave fields), and it becomes decisive for $p\gtrsim12$
($e_\delta$ of the monomials $\ge2\times10^{-9}$ on every test element, up to
$3\times10^{-2}$ at $p=16$).

\paragraph{Cost.} All bases are evaluated with vectorized recurrences or
products; the Lagrange basis costs $\mathcal O(p^2)$ instead of $\mathcal O(p)$
operations per 1D function and point. Measured in GNU Octave, a call of
\code{ShapeFunctions2D_Metric} with the $(2p)^2$ Gauss points takes
$0.5$--$1.9$ times as long as with monomials (no systematic trend with $p$);
the solve with the $(p+1)^2\times(p+1)^2$ matrix and the evaluation of the
parametric functions dominate the element routines in either case.

\section{Choice and implementation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Default: Lagrange basis of the reference element in
    bounding-box scaled skew coordinates} (\code{p.metricBasis = 'lagrange'}). It
    is the only candidate that is exact on affine elements ($\matr V$ a
    permutation matrix, so metric and parametric functions coincide to
    round-off and $\matr K_e$ is symmetric to round-off for all $p$), it has the
    interpretation of Proposition~2, and on distorted elements it is as accurate as
    the orthogonal bases. It uses the node distribution \code{p.ndist} of the
    element, so that $\matr V$ is a permutation matrix for every distribution.
  \item \code{'legendre'} and \code{'chebyshev'} are retained for comparison;
    they are marginally better conditioned on the distorted test elements
    ($\le$ factor 3) and not exact on affine ones.
  \item \code{'monomial'} reproduces the previous implementation (no scaling). It
    is adequate for $p\lesssim6$ ($e_\delta<5\times10^{-13}$) and should not be used
    for $p\gtrsim10$.
  \item \code{method = 0} (dimensional global coordinates,
    \cite{Rajendran2003novel}) always uses monomials; the reference interval has
    no meaning there.
  \item Nothing changes for the existing tests and results within round-off:
    the full test suite (patch tests, Lee \& Bathe benchmarks, element matrices,
    time integrators) passes unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitation and outlook: the Lebesgue constant of
\texorpdfstring{$Q_p(s,t)$}{Qp(s,t)}}
\label{sec:metric-basis-outlook}

\Cref{fig:metric-basis-leb} shows that the Lebesgue constant $\Lambda_M$ of the
metric interpolation grows exponentially with $p$ on distorted elements, while
that of the parametric interpolation, $\Lambda_N$, grows only logarithmically
($\Lambda_N\approx6.1$ at $p=16$). This is a property of the space $Q_p(s,t)$
on the mapped node set and is independent of the basis
(Proposition~1). Its consequences:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Round-off floor.} Perturbations of nodal values are amplified by
    up to $\Lambda_M$, and by \cref{eq:kappa-lagrange} the shape functions carry
    errors of order $\Lambda_M u$ even with the best basis. On the trapezoid of
    \code{usemTestElement} ($|s|\le1.27$), $\Lambda_M\approx8\times10^2$ at $p=8$
    and $4\times10^7$ at $p=16$.
  \item \textbf{Approximation.} For fields outside the trial space the
    interpolation error bound contains the factor $1+\Lambda_M$. Whether the
    Petrov--Galerkin solution inherits this depends on the discrete inf-sup
    constant, which is not analysed here; the mesh-level results in
    \cref{sec:metric-basis-results} show no deterioration up to $p=12$ on the
    mildly distorted test mesh.
\end{itemize}
The growth is not a consequence of Cartesian completeness. The hybrid space
\begin{equation}
  T_p = P_p(x,y)\;\oplus\;\operatorname{span}\{L_a(\xi)L_b(\eta):\ a,b\le p,\
  a+b>p\}
\end{equation}
has the same dimension $(p+1)^2$, contains $P_p(x,y)$ (so Rajendran's
completeness condition and the patch tests are unaffected) and is frame invariant,
but its high-order complement is written in the parametric coordinates, in which
the nodes form a tensor grid. \code{probe_hybrid_space.m} gives a Lebesgue constant
close to the parametric one on all distorted test elements
(\cref{fig:metric-basis-hybrid}): at $p=16$, $6.0$ instead of $4.0\times10^7$
(trapezoid), $6.1$ instead of $5.5\times10^4$ (curved) and $8.5$ instead of
$6.7\times10^6$ (general). On affine elements $T_p=Q_p$. Using $T_p$ would change the
element, not only its numerics: the trial functions are no longer polynomials in
$(x,y)$, their evaluation needs the parametric coordinates of the query points
(available in all element routines, which evaluate at mapped Gauss points), and
accuracy, stability and the dynamic behaviour would have to be re-assessed. It is
therefore not implemented; it is the most promising follow-up of this study.
