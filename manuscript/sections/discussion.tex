\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What each improvement achieves}
The two improvements act on different error sources and should not be conflated.
The Lagrange basis of \cref{sec:basis} removes the ill-conditioning \emph{of the
construction}: it is exact on affine elements and, by \cref{prop:kappa}, adds no
amplification beyond that of the interpolation problem on distorted ones. It cannot
change the shape functions (\cref{prop:invariance}) and hence cannot change the
Lebesgue constant, the patch-test behaviour in exact arithmetic, the eigenvalues or
the discrete solution beyond round-off. The hybrid space of \cref{sec:hybrid} changes
the \emph{space}, and with it the Lebesgue constant, the stability properties and the
approximation constant. Both are needed: the hybrid space has its own Vandermonde
matrix, which is well conditioned in the Legendre-type basis used here because the
complement is evaluated on the exact GLL tensor grid (\cref{fig:kappa}).

\paragraph{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item No a priori stability (discrete inf-sup) estimate is available for either metric
  space; the statements on stability rest on the computed spectra, condition numbers
  and convergence histories. \Cref{prop:representation} gives exact representations and
  upper bounds of the Lebesgue constants, but the size of the correction operators is
  quantified numerically only.
\item The completeness degree of $\Tp_p$ is $\min(p_\xi,p_\eta)$, the same as for
  $\Qp_{p_\xi,p_\eta}(s,t)$; neither space reproduces $\Pp_{\max(p_\xi,p_\eta)}$.
\item The complement contributes rational integrands to $\matr K_e$ and
  $\matr M_e$; Gauss quadrature is then not exact, as for curved isoparametric
  elements. Where $\det\matr J$ vanishes inside an element, the integrands are singular.
\item The study is restricted to linear plane elasticity on quadrilaterals with at most
  biquadratic geometry. The construction carries over to hexahedra
  ($\Pp_p(x,y,z)\oplus\Cp_p$ with the $L^2$-complement of $\Pp_p(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$ in
  $\Qp_p(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$) and to triangles (complement in a simplex basis), but has not
  been tested there.
\end{itemize}
