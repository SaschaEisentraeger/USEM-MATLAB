\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Two improvements of the metric trial functions of unsymmetric spectral elements were
presented, analysed and tested.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Basis of the metric Vandermonde matrix.} The metric shape functions are
  basis independent. The Lagrange polynomials through the reference GLL nodes, in
  bounding-box scaled skew coordinates, give $\cond(\matr V)=1$ on affine elements; on
  distorted ones the condition number equals the Lebesgue function of the metric
  interpolation on the reference grid of the bounding box (up to a bounded factor).
  Compared with monomials this reduces the error of the computed shape functions by two to
  four orders of magnitude on distorted elements at $p=16$ and restores the symmetry of
  $\matr K_e$ on affine elements to round-off. The Lebesgue
  constant, and with it everything that depends only on the space, is unaffected by
  any choice of basis.
\item \emph{Hybrid trial space.} Writing only $\Pp_p(x,y)$ in metric coordinates and the
  canonical ($L^2$-orthogonal) complement in parametric coordinates retains completeness,
  affine invariance and distortion immunity, reduces to the classical element on
  straight-edged quadrilaterals, and keeps the Lebesgue constant at or close to the GLL
  value for moderate curved-edge distortion. In the tests it removed the divergence under
  mesh refinement and the negative and strongly complex eigenvalues of the $\Qp_p(s,t)$
  element, reduced the exponential growth in time by orders of magnitude (a slow residual
  growth remains), and converged with the optimal order on non-folded meshes; for the thick
  cylinder its error was $0.78$--$1.00$ times its interpolation error on all valid meshes.
  Unisolvence reduces to the non-singularity of a $\dim\Pp_m\times\dim\Pp_m$ matrix, which
  fails only on some folded elements at high degree. The hybrid space trades this
  robustness for a moderately larger error constant on smooth static problems where
  $\Qp_p(s,t)$ is stable.
\end{enumerate}
For high-order unsymmetric spectral elements we therefore recommend the reference-node
Lagrange basis for any metric space in skew coordinates, and the hybrid space whenever
$p\gtrsim4$, the curved-edge distortion is not small, or the spectrum matters (transient,
modal and frequency-domain analyses).
Open questions are a stability theory (discrete inf-sup condition) for both metric spaces,
an accurate treatment of the singular integrands of the hybrid space on folded elements,
the residual (slow) growth of the hybrid element in long-time transient analysis, and the
extension to three dimensions, triangles and nonlinear problems.
