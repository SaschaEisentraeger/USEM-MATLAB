\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%
In the article at hand, two improvements of the metric trial functions of unsymmetric spectral elements have been presented, analyzed and tested. They address the loss of accuracy on strongly distorted elements that was observed for the axisymmetric unsymmetric spectral element in the companion paper~\cite{Eisentraeger2026axisymmetric}, and they separate its two causes, namely the conditioning of the construction and the interpolation problem itself. The most important conclusions that can be drawn from the presented examples are summarized at this point:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The metric shape functions do not depend on the basis in which they are constructed. Therefore, the Lagrange polynomials through the GLL points of the reference square in bounding-box scaled skew coordinates can be employed, for which the Vandermonde matrix of a parallelogram is a permutation matrix. On distorted elements, the condition number is governed by the values of the metric shape functions at the GLL grid of the bounding box, i.e., by their extrapolation beyond the element. Compared with monomials, the error of the computed shape functions on distorted elements at $p=16$ is reduced by two to four orders of magnitude, and the symmetry of the stiffness matrix on parallelograms is restored to round-off. The Lebesgue constant, and with it everything that depends only on the trial space, is unaffected by any choice of basis.
  \item Writing only the complete polynomials of degree $p$ in skew coordinates and the remaining functions, the Legendre products orthogonal to them, in the reference coordinates retains completeness, the independence of the element position and orientation and the exact reproduction of polynomial solutions. The hybrid trial space reduces to the conventional element on straight-sided quadrilaterals, and its Lebesgue constant stays at or close to the GLL value for moderate edge curvature.
  \item In the numerical tests, the hybrid trial space removed the divergence under mesh refinement and the eigenvalues with negative real part of the original metric trial space, and it reduced the growth of the transient response by up to four orders of magnitude, although a slow residual growth remains. On meshes without folded elements, it converged with the optimal order, and for the thick cylinder its error was $0.78$ to $1.00$ times its interpolation error.
  \item The existence of the hybrid shape functions depends only on a small matrix of the size of the number of complete polynomials, and it was lost only on some folded elements for $p\ge7$. On folded elements, however, the stiffness integrals of the hybrid trial space do not exist, as those of the conventional element, so that it is restricted to meshes without folded elements.
  \item The price for this robustness is an error constant that is $1.3$ to $5.6$ times larger for smooth static problems on which the original metric trial space is stable.
\end{enumerate}

Overall, we recommend the reference-node Lagrange basis for every metric trial space in skew coordinates of unsymmetric spectral elements of high degree. The hybrid trial space is recommended whenever $p\gtrsim4$, the edge curvature is not small or the spectrum matters, as in transient, modal and frequency-domain analyses. Several questions are still open and left for future research: a stability theory (a discrete inf-sup condition) for both metric trial spaces, a hybrid trial space whose stiffness integrals exist on folded elements, the slow residual growth in long-time transient analyses, and the extension to three dimensions, triangles and nonlinear problems. Moreover, the hybrid trial space has to be put to the test in the axisymmetric setting of the companion paper, together with its enrichment, on the angularly distorted meshes and on the pressure vessel, for which the original metric trial functions lost accuracy.
