\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In the article at hand, two improvements of the metric trial functions of unsymmetric
spectral elements have been presented, analysed and tested. The most important
conclusions can be summarised as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Basis of the metric Vandermonde matrix.} The metric shape functions are
  basis independent. Therefore, the Lagrange polynomials through the reference GLL nodes,
  in bounding-box scaled skew coordinates, can be employed, which give
  $\cond(\matr V)=1$ on affine elements. On distorted ones, the condition number equals
  the Lebesgue function of the metric interpolation on the reference grid of the bounding
  box (up to a bounded factor). Compared with monomials, this reduces the error of the
  computed shape functions by two to four orders of magnitude on distorted elements at
  $p=16$ and restores the symmetry of $\matr K_e$ on affine elements to round-off. The
  Lebesgue constant, and with it everything that depends only on the space, is
  unaffected by any choice of basis.
\item \emph{Hybrid trial space.} Writing only $\Pp_p(x,y)$ in metric coordinates and the
  canonical ($L^2$-orthogonal) complement in parametric coordinates retains completeness,
  affine invariance and distortion immunity. Moreover, the hybrid space reduces to the
  classical element on straight-edged quadrilaterals and keeps the Lebesgue constant at or
  close to the GLL value for moderate curved-edge distortion. In the numerical tests, it
  removed the divergence under mesh refinement and the negative and strongly complex
  eigenvalues of the $\Qp_p(s,t)$ element. It also reduced the exponential growth in time
  by orders of magnitude, although a slow residual growth remains. On non-folded meshes,
  it converged with the optimal order, and for the thick cylinder its error was
  $0.78$--$1.00$ times its interpolation error on all valid meshes. Unisolvence reduces to
  the non-singularity of a $\dim\Pp_m\times\dim\Pp_m$ matrix, which fails only on some
  folded elements at high degree. The price for this robustness is a moderately larger
  error constant on smooth static problems where $\Qp_p(s,t)$ is stable.
\end{enumerate}
Overall, we therefore recommend the reference-node Lagrange basis for any metric space in
skew coordinates of high-order unsymmetric spectral elements. The hybrid space is
recommended whenever $p\gtrsim4$, the curved-edge distortion is not small or the spectrum
matters (transient, modal and frequency-domain analyses).
Several questions are still open and left for future research: a stability theory
(discrete inf-sup condition) for both metric spaces, an accurate treatment of the singular
integrands of the hybrid space on folded elements, the residual (slow) growth of the hybrid
element in long-time transient analysis and the extension to three dimensions, triangles
and nonlinear problems.
