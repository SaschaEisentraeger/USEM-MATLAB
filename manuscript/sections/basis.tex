\section{Improvement 1: the basis of the metric Vandermonde matrix}
\label{sec:basis}

\subsection{Basis invariance and floating-point error}

\begin{proposition}[Basis invariance]
\label{prop:invariance}
If $\matr V$ in \eqref{eq:vandermonde} is non-singular, the metric functions are the
unique functions $M_l^e\in\mathcal U_e$ with $M_l^e(\bx_k)=\delta_{lk}$; they do not
depend on the basis $\{\phi_c\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For another basis $\bm\psi=\matr G\bm\phi$ with $\matr G$ non-singular,
$\matr V_\psi=\matr G\matr V_\phi$ and $\matr P_\psi=\matr G\matr P_\phi$, so
$\matr V_\psi^{-1}\matr P_\psi=\matr V_\phi^{-1}\matr P_\phi$. Uniqueness follows from
the non-singularity of $\matr V$.
\end{proof}

Completeness, affine invariance, distortion immunity and unisolvence are therefore
properties of the space and the node set alone, and so is the Lebesgue constant
\eqref{eq:lebesgue}. The basis affects only the floating-point error. For the
solution of $\matr V\matr N=\matr P$ by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting
the standard perturbation bound \citep{Higham2002Accuracy} gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\|\hat{\matr N}-\matr N\|}{\|\matr N\|}\lesssim c_n\,\rho_n\,\cond(\matr V)\,u ,
  \label{eq:fp}
\end{equation}
with the growth factor $\rho_n$ (moderate in practice) and $u\approx1.1\times10^{-16}$.
The objective of the basis is thus to minimise $\cond(\matr V)$.

\subsection{Candidate bases and bounding-box scaling}

We consider the tensor-product bases $\phi_c(\vect s)=\varphi_a(\tilde s)\varphi_b(\tilde t)$,
$0\le a,b\le p$, with
\begin{itemize}
\item monomials $\varphi_a(s)=s^a$ (the original construction);
\item Legendre polynomials $\varphi_a=L_a$ and Chebyshev polynomials $\varphi_a=T_a$,
  evaluated by their three-term recurrences;
\item the Lagrange polynomials $\varphi_a=\ell_a$ through the $p+1$ GLL points
  $\hat s_0<\dots<\hat s_p$ of $[-1,1]$, i.e.\ the 1D factors of the reference shape
  functions, evaluated by the product formula.
\end{itemize}
The last three bases are designed for $[-1,1]$, but on distorted elements the skew
coordinates of the nodes leave $[-1,1]^2$; outside this interval the polynomials
grow rapidly ($|T_p(s)|=\cosh(p\operatorname{arcosh}|s|)$ for $|s|>1$). We therefore
map the bounding box of the nodes in $(s,t)$ onto $[-1,1]^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\tilde s=\frac{s-c_s}{r_s},\qquad \tilde t=\frac{t-c_t}{r_t},\\
  &c_s=\tfrac12\bigl(\max_k s_k+\min_k s_k\bigr),\quad
  r_s=\tfrac12\bigl(\max_k s_k-\min_k s_k\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:bbox}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $t$. The map is affine and computed from affine-invariant
quantities, so the space $\Qp_p(s,t)$, completeness and the invariance of
\cref{lem:affine} are preserved. On a parallelogram the nodes satisfy $\vect s_k=\bxi_k$,
whose bounding box is $\hat\Omega$, so \eqref{eq:bbox} is the identity there. The
derivatives follow from $\nabla_{\bx}=\matr J_0^{-1}\,\mathrm{diag}(r_s^{-1},r_t^{-1})\,
\nabla_{\tilde{\vect s}}$.

\subsection{Affine elements}

On a parallelogram the nodes form the GLL tensor grid in $(\tilde s,\tilde t)$, so
$\matr V$ is, up to a permutation of the columns, the Kronecker product
$\matr V_1\otimes\matr V_1$ of the 1D Vandermonde matrix, and
$\cond_2(\matr V)=\cond_2(\matr V_1)^2$. For monomials on nodes symmetric about the
origin, $\cond(\matr V_1)$ grows at least exponentially \citep{Gautschi1988Lower},
so the 2D condition number grows with the square of an exponential. For the
Lagrange basis $\phi_c(\vect s_k)=\delta_{ck}$ up to the ordering:

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:affine}
On an affine element, the Lagrange basis gives a permutation matrix $\matr V$ (up to
the rounding of the scaled skew coordinates of the nodes), $\cond_q(\matr V)=1$ for
every $q$-norm, and $\matr N=\matr V^{-1}\matr P$ is obtained without amplification of
rounding errors. Metric and parametric
functions then agree to round-off, and $\matr K_e$ is symmetric to round-off, for
every $p$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Distorted elements}

On a non-affine element the conditioning of $\matr V$ in the Lagrange basis has a
precise interpretation. Let $\hat{\vect s}_d$, $d=1,\dots,n$, denote the reference
tensor grid in the scaled skew coordinates, so that $\phi_c(\hat{\vect s}_d)=\delta_{cd}$,
and let $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}(\tilde{\vect s})=\sum_c|\phi_c(\tilde{\vect s})|$ be the
Lebesgue function of the reference interpolation.

\begin{proposition}[Condition number in the Lagrange basis]
\label{prop:kappa}
With the Lagrange basis and $\tilde{\vect s}_k$ the scaled skew coordinates of the
element nodes,
\begin{equation}
  (\matr V^{-1})_{ld}=M_l^e(\hat{\vect s}_d),\qquad
  \cond_1(\matr V)=\max_k\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}(\tilde{\vect s}_k)\;
                   \max_d\lambda_{\mathcal U}(\hat{\vect s}_d) ,
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
where $M_l^e$ and $\lambda_{\mathcal U}$ are understood as functions of the skew
coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choosing the query points $\hat{\vect s}_d$ in \eqref{eq:vandermonde} gives
$\matr P=\matr I$ and hence $\matr N=\matr V^{-1}$, which is the first identity. The
1-norm is the maximum absolute column sum: $\|\matr V\|_1=\max_k\sum_c|\phi_c(\tilde{\vect s}_k)|$
and $\|\matr V^{-1}\|_1=\max_d\sum_l|M_l^e(\hat{\vect s}_d)|$.
\end{proof}

By \eqref{eq:bbox}, all $\tilde{\vect s}_k\in\hat\Omega$, so the first factor is
bounded by $\Lam_N$ (e.g.\ $\Lam_N\le6.1$ for $p\le16$). The second factor is the Lebesgue
function of the metric interpolation at the reference tensor grid of the bounding box.
On an affine element this grid coincides with the nodes and the factor is $1$. On a
distorted element, however, part of the grid lies \emph{outside} the element (e.g.\ the
corners of the bounding box), where $\lambda_{\mathcal U}$ is an extrapolation and can
exceed the Lebesgue constant $\Lam_{\mathcal U}$ by orders of magnitude
(\cref{sec:num-conditioning}). \Cref{prop:kappa} thus identifies the source of the
remaining ill-conditioning of every tensor-product basis on the bounding box: the
extrapolation of the metric interpolant from the element to its bounding box. It cannot
be removed by switching among the bases considered here (they lie within a factor of
about~3 of one another numerically), only by bases adapted to the node set itself, e.g.\
discretely orthogonalised bases \citep{Brubeck2021Vandermonde}, which we do not pursue.

The proposition also shows the limit of \emph{any} basis: by \cref{prop:invariance},
$\lambda_{\mathcal U}$ and $\Lam_{\mathcal U}$ do not depend on the basis, and a large
Lebesgue constant amplifies perturbations of the nodal values (round-off, or the
nodal error of a discrete solution) by up to $\Lam_{\mathcal U}$, whatever basis is
used. Reducing $\Lam_{\mathcal U}$ requires a different \emph{space}, which is the
subject of \cref{sec:hybrid}.

\begin{remark}[Reproduction versus generic data]
\label{rem:reproduction}
For nodal data $\vect f=\matr V^\T\vect c$ of a function in $\mathcal U_e$ with
coefficient vector $\vect c$, the first-order error of the computed interpolant is
$\vect c^\T\delta\matr V\,\matr N$, with the backward error $\delta\matr V$ of the
factorisation, and is bounded by $u\,\|\vect c\|\,\|\matr V\|\,\Lam_{\mathcal U}$ up
to a constant. For low-degree polynomials $\|\vect c\|\|\matr V\|$ is moderate in
every basis, so the \emph{reproduction} of polynomials (and patch tests) is
insensitive to $\cond(\matr V)$ and limited by $u\Lam_{\mathcal U}$. Generic nodal
data (unit vectors, eigenvectors, wave fields) can have
$\|\vect c\|\approx\|\matr V^{-1}\|\|\vect f\|$; the error is then of order
$\cond(\matr V)u$. The element matrices are built from the individual functions
$M_l^e$, i.e.\ from unit nodal data, and therefore inherit \eqref{eq:fp}.
\end{remark}
