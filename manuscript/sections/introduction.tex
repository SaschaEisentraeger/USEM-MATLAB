\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Isoparametric finite elements lose accuracy on distorted meshes because the
isoparametric map destroys the completeness of the approximation space in
Cartesian coordinates: under angular distortion, serendipity elements no longer
contain all quadratic polynomials \citep{Lee1993effects,Rajendran2000completeness},
and under curved-edge distortion even full tensor-product (Lagrange) elements of
degree $p$ with a geometry map of degree $g$ are guaranteed to contain $\Pp_k(x,y)$ only
for $gk\le p$ \citep[cf.][]{Arnold2002Approximation}.
MacNeal's theorem \citep{MacNeal1992On} shows that, within the class of symmetric
(Bubnov--Galerkin) elements, distortion insensitivity and passing the patch test
cannot be combined in general. The unsymmetric finite element method (UFEM) of
\citet{Rajendran2003novel} circumvents this restriction by a Petrov--Galerkin
formulation: the test functions are the usual parametric (isoparametric)
functions, which guarantee inter-element compatibility, whereas the trial
functions are \emph{metric} functions, i.e.\ polynomials in Cartesian
coordinates that are interpolatory at the element nodes. \Citet{Rajendran2010technique}
formulated three sufficient conditions for \emph{distortion immunity}:
compatible test functions, trial functions that are complete in Cartesian
coordinates, and an element stiffness integrand free of $\det\matr J$ in the
denominator. Elements satisfying them reproduce every displacement field of the
completeness degree exactly on arbitrarily distorted meshes, including meshes with
$\det\matr J\le0$. The approach has been extended to frame-invariant formulations
in skew coordinates \citep{Yuan1993New,Ooi2008Remedies,Xie2016Modified}, to
analytical trial functions \citep{Cen2015unsymmetric,Cen2019Some}, to solids
\citep{Ooi2004hexahedron}, to geometrically nonlinear problems
\citep{Li2018high}, and to elastodynamics
\citep{Liew2006quadratic,Wang2022Temporal,Zaehringer2026On}; the
non-variational character of the formulation was analysed by
\citet{Prathap2006mesh,Prathap2007The}.

Almost all of this work concerns low-order elements (4 to 20 nodes). We consider
the generalisation to spectral elements of arbitrary degree $p$: parametric test
functions are Lagrange polynomials on Gauss--Lobatto--Legendre (GLL) nodes, and the
metric trial functions are the unique functions of a $(p+1)^2$-dimensional space
that interpolate the GLL nodes in physical space. In the standard construction the
metric space is $\Qp_p(s,t)$, the tensor-product polynomials in the non-dimensional
corotational skew coordinates $(s,t)$ of \citet{Xie2016Modified}, and the shape
functions are obtained by inverting a generalised Vandermonde matrix built from the
monomials $s^at^b$ \citep{Rajendran2003novel,Ooi2006unsymmetric}. Two difficulties
appear at high order, and they are of different nature:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Ill-conditioning of the construction.} The monomial Vandermonde matrix
  is exponentially ill-conditioned in $p$ already on undistorted elements
  \citep{Gautschi1988Lower}. The computed shape functions then carry round-off errors
  of order $\cond(\matr V)\,u$, $u$ being the unit round-off, which destroy, for
  instance, the symmetry of the stiffness matrix on affine elements and the
  Kronecker property on which the diagonal GLL mass matrix relies.
\item \emph{Ill-conditioning of the interpolation problem itself.} On non-affine
  elements the images of the GLL nodes do not form a tensor grid in $(s,t)$. The
  Lebesgue constant of the interpolation in $\Qp_p(s,t)$ then grows exponentially with
  $p$ and with the amount of distortion. It is a property of the space and of the
  node set, independent of how the shape functions are computed. It amplifies
  round-off, enters the interpolation error, and---as we show---is accompanied by a
  loss of stability of the Petrov--Galerkin operator: strongly complex and negative
  generalised eigenvalues, which make the semi-discrete equations of motion
  exponentially unstable, and divergence under mesh refinement in statics.
\end{enumerate}

This paper presents two improvements of the metric shape functions that address
these two difficulties separately.

\paragraph{Contribution 1: polynomial basis of the metric Vandermonde matrix
(\cref{sec:basis})} We show that the metric shape functions are independent of
the polynomial basis used to construct them (\cref{prop:invariance}), so that the
basis is a purely numerical choice, and we propose the Lagrange polynomials through
the reference GLL nodes in bounding-box scaled skew coordinates. On affine elements
the Vandermonde matrix becomes a permutation matrix ($\cond=1$ for every $p$); on
distorted elements its condition number equals, up to a factor bounded by the
reference Lebesgue constant, the Lebesgue function of the metric interpolation on the
reference grid of the bounding box (\cref{prop:kappa}), which traces the remaining
ill-conditioning to the extrapolation of the interpolant beyond the element. Numerically,
the condition number at $p=16$ drops by three to four orders of magnitude relative to
monomials. The same analysis shows that \emph{no} choice of basis can reduce the
Lebesgue constant.

\paragraph{Contribution 2: hybrid metric--parametric trial space
(\cref{sec:hybrid})} Distortion immunity requires only that the trial space
contain the complete polynomials $\Pp_p(x,y)$, i.e.\ $(p+1)(p+2)/2$ of the
$(p+1)^2$ functions. We propose the trial space
\[
  \Tp_p = \Pp_p(x,y)\big|_{\Omega_e}\;\oplus\;\Cp_p\circ\vect F_e^{-1},
\]
in which the complete polynomials are written in metric (skew) coordinates and the
remaining approximation, $\Cp_p$, is the $L^2$-orthogonal complement of
$\Pp_p(\xi,\eta)$ in $\Qp_p(\xi,\eta)$, written in the parametric coordinates, in
which the nodes form an exact tensor grid. We prove that $\Tp_p$ is complete,
affine invariant and interpolatory, that it coincides with the classical spectral
element space on every straight-edged quadrilateral, and we derive an exact
representation of its interpolation operator as the GLL interpolant plus a
correction that is driven only by the completeness defect of the classical element
(\cref{prop:representation}). Numerically, its Lebesgue constant stays at the level
of GLL interpolation for moderate curved-edge distortion and remains orders of
magnitude below that of $\Qp_p(s,t)$ for strong distortion. On all meshes tested,
the eigenvalues with negative real part of the $\Qp_p(s,t)$ element disappear and the
relative imaginary parts drop from $\mathcal O(1)$ to below $2\cdot10^{-2}$.

The numerical studies (\cref{sec:numerics}) quantify conditioning, accuracy of the
shape functions, Lebesgue constants versus degree and distortion, patch tests on
distorted and folded meshes, $h$-convergence on meshes whose distortion persists
under refinement, eigenvalue spectra and transient stability. They also document
where the hybrid space is \emph{less} accurate than $\Qp_p(s,t)$ (static problems
with smooth solutions at moderate degree and distortion), so that the trade-off is
explicit. The paper is restricted to linear plane elasticity on quadrilaterals.
