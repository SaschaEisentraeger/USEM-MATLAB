\section{Test elements and meshes}
\label{app:elements}
%
The single test elements are defined by 4- or 8-node input elements (corner nodes counter-clockwise, followed by the mid-side nodes of the edges 1--2, 2--3, 3--4 and 4--1) and lifted to degree $p$ by isoparametric interpolation of the input geometry:

    curved & $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(2,1)$, $(0,1)$; $(1,-0.2)$, $(2.25,0.5)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,0.5)$ \\
    general & $(0,0)$, $(2,0.1)$, $(1.5,1.2)$, $(0.2,1)$; $(1.1,-0.15)$, $(1.9,0.6)$, $(0.8,1.25)$, $(0.05,0.5)$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}
The meshes of \cref{sec:num-convergence} consist of $N\times N$ 8-node elements on the unit square whose interior mid-side nodes are moved normal to their edge by $\delta h$ with $h=1/N$, so that the relative sagitta of every interior edge is $\delta$. The normal is oriented consistently, so that all interior horizontal edges bow in the $-y$-direction and all interior vertical edges in the $-x$-direction. The $3\times2$ meshes of \cref{sec:num-patch,sec:num-spectra} on $[0,3]\times[0,2]$ use the same curved-edge distortion with $\delta=0.15$ and $0.20$ (with $h=1$). For the mesh denoted by \emph{angular + curved $0.30$}, the interior corner nodes are first shifted alternately by $\pm0.3$ in the $x$-direction, before the curved-edge distortion with $\delta=0.15$ is applied.

\section{Coordinates of the complete polynomials}
\label{app:frames}
%
Since all affine coordinates span the same space $\Pp_p(x,y)$, the coordinates of the first group in \cref{eq:hybrid} do not affect the hybrid shape functions, but only the conditioning of $\matr V_{\mathrm{T}}$. The singular matrices of \PH\ in \cref{tab:unisolvence} are a consequence of the skew coordinates: on folded elements, the Jacobian matrix at the element center is poorly conditioned, and the skew coordinates of the nodes almost collapse onto a line. We have therefore tested two alternatives that do not rely on the Jacobian matrix at a single point, both with the bounding-box scaling \cref{eq:bbox}. The first are the principal axes of the nodes, obtained from a singular value decomposition of the node coordinates relative to their centroid $\bx_{\mathrm{m}}$. The second is the affine least-squares fit of the reference coordinates to the nodes, i.e., the coordinates $\matr B(\bx-\bx_{\mathrm{m}})$, where the matrix $\matr B$ minimizes $\sum_j\lVert\matr B(\bx_j-\bx_{\mathrm{m}})-(\xi_j,\eta_j)^\top\rVert^2$.

As shown in \cref{tab:frames}, both alternatives remove all singular matrices on the thick-cylinder meshes. The principal axes, however, do not preserve the tensor-product grid of the nodes on affine elements, which degrades the conditioning on regular elements. On the parallelogram at $p=16$, e.g., the error of the Kronecker property increases from $1.1\cdot10^{-14}$ to $2.1\cdot10^{-10}$. The least-squares fit reproduces the reference coordinates exactly on affine elements and therefore does not have this drawback. With the principal axes, we repeated the studies of \cref{sec:num-cylinder}: on the meshes without folded elements, the errors of \PH\ changed only at the round-off level. The least-squares fit has only been evaluated in terms of the Vandermonde matrix.

The benefit of both alternatives is thus limited to folded elements. There, the shape functions exist, but the stiffness integrals of \PH\ still do not (\cref{sec:hybrid-gradients}), so that its errors continue to depend on the quadrature rule. We therefore retain the skew coordinates, which \PH\ shares with \PM.
