\begin{abstract}
Unsymmetric (Petrov--Galerkin) finite elements with parametric test and metric trial
functions reproduce all polynomial displacement fields of the completeness degree
exactly on arbitrarily distorted meshes. For spectral elements of high degree $p$,
the standard metric trial space---tensor-product polynomials $\Qp_p(s,t)$ in corotational
skew coordinates, constructed by inverting a monomial Vandermonde matrix---suffers from
two distinct deficiencies: an exponentially ill-conditioned construction, and an
interpolation problem whose Lebesgue constant grows exponentially with $p$ and with
the distortion. We propose one remedy for each. (1) The metric shape functions are
independent of the polynomial basis; using the Lagrange polynomials through the
reference Gauss--Lobatto--Legendre nodes in bounding-box scaled skew coordinates makes
the Vandermonde matrix a permutation matrix on affine elements and reduces the
condition number on distorted elements by three to four orders of magnitude at $p=16$
compared with monomials. We prove that its condition number equals the Lebesgue function
of the metric interpolation on the reference grid of the element's bounding box, which
identifies the remaining ill-conditioning as an extrapolation effect, and that no basis
can reduce the Lebesgue constant itself. (2) We introduce a hybrid trial space in which only the complete polynomials
$\Pp_p(x,y)$, which are required for distortion immunity, are written in metric
coordinates, while the remaining approximation is the $L^2$-orthogonal complement of
$\Pp_p(\xi,\eta)$ in $\Qp_p(\xi,\eta)$, written in parametric coordinates. The space is
complete, affine invariant and interpolatory, coincides with the classical spectral
element on straight-edged quadrilaterals, and its interpolant is the GLL interpolant
plus a correction driven only by the completeness defect of the classical element;
unisolvence is equivalent to the non-singularity of a small matrix of the size of
$\dim\Pp_p$.
Numerical studies up to $p=16$ show condition numbers reduced by up to five orders of
magnitude, Lebesgue constants at or close to the GLL level for moderate curved-edge
distortion where those of $\Qp_p(s,t)$ reach $10^{4}$--$10^{7}$, patch tests on non-folded
meshes passed at round-off level up to $p=10$,
optimal convergence on meshes with persistent curved-edge distortion where the
$\Qp_p(s,t)$ element diverges for $p\ge5$, errors up to $390$ times smaller than with
$\Qp_p(s,t)$ for the distorted thick-cylinder benchmark, and the removal of the strongly complex and
negative eigenvalues that make the $\Qp_p(s,t)$ element exponentially unstable in
transient analysis. The price is a larger error constant (factor $1.3$--$5.5$) for smooth
static solutions where $\Qp_p(s,t)$ is stable, and, on folded elements, singular
integrands that limit the attainable accuracy.
\end{abstract}
