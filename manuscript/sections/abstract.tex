\begin{abstract}
Unsymmetric (Petrov-Galerkin) spectral elements combine conforming parametric test functions with metric trial functions that are complete in the physical coordinates. On meshes whose edge curvature persists under refinement, they therefore converge optimally, whereas the conventional spectral element of degree $p$ with a quadratic geometry is complete only to degree $\lfloor p/2\rfloor$. However, the metric trial functions, obtained by nodal interpolation in the tensor-product polynomials of skew coordinates, overshoot between the nodes on strongly distorted elements, increasingly with the degree. Here, we separate two causes of this behavior and propose one remedy for each. First, to improve the conditioning, we use the Lagrange polynomials through the reference Gauss-Lobatto-Legendre (GLL) points, scaled to the bounding box of the nodes, as the basis of the generalized Vandermonde matrix. At $p=16$, its condition number is then three to four orders of magnitude smaller than with monomials, although no basis can reduce the overshoot itself. Second, to improve the interpolation problem, we write only the complete polynomials of degree $p$ in skew coordinates and the remaining functions, Legendre products orthogonal to them, in the reference coordinates, in which the nodes form the exact GLL grid. The resulting hybrid element coincides with the conventional one on straight-sided quadrilaterals. For moderate edge curvature, its Lebesgue constant, i.e., the largest overshoot factor, stays close to the GLL value, whereas that of the original metric space reaches $10^4$--$10^7$ at $p=16$. On meshes with persistent curvature, the hybrid element converges optimally for $p\le6$, whereas the original element diverges for $p=5,6$. For a distorted thick cylinder, its error is up to 750 times smaller and close to the interpolation error of its trial space. Moreover, the eigenvalues with negative real part are removed. The price is an error that is 1.3 to 5.6 times larger where the original element is stable.
\end{abstract}
