\begin{abstract}
Unsymmetric (Petrov-Galerkin) spectral elements combine conforming parametric test functions with metric trial functions complete in physical coordinates. Therefore, they converge optimally on meshes with persistent edge curvature, where the conventional spectral element of degree $p$ with quadratic geometry is complete only to degree $\lfloor p/2\rfloor$. However, their metric trial functions overshoot on strongly distorted elements, increasingly with the degree. We separate two causes of this behavior and propose one remedy for each. First, the generalized Vandermonde matrix is set up in the Lagrange basis through the reference Gauss-Lobatto-Legendre (GLL) points, scaled to the nodal bounding box. At $p=16$, this reduces its condition number by three to four orders of magnitude versus monomials, although no basis can reduce the overshoot itself. Second, only the complete polynomials of degree $p$ are written in skew coordinates, the remaining functions (Legendre products orthogonal to them) in the reference coordinates, where the nodes form the exact GLL grid. This hybrid element reduces to the conventional one on straight-sided quadrilaterals. For moderate edge curvature, its Lebesgue constant (largest overshoot factor) stays close to the GLL value, compared with $10^4$--$10^7$ for the original metric space at $p=16$. On meshes with persistent curvature, it converges optimally for $p\le6$, whereas the original one diverges for $p=5,6$. It also removes eigenvalues with negative real part and reduces the error for a distorted thick cylinder up to 750-fold. The price is a 1.3 to 5.6 times larger error where the original element is stable.
\end{abstract}
