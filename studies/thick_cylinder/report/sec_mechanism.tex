The diagnostics in \texttt{results/} identify where the loss of \PM\ originates and why \PH\
avoids it.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Not the Jacobian, not quadrature.} The loss occurs on meshes with
  $\det(\bm J)>0$ ($\alpha=30^\circ$) and persists with exact integration
  (\cref{sec:quad,sec:poly}).
\item \emph{Not consistency, not approximation.} The residual of the exact nodal values,
  $\bm r=\bm K\bm u_{\mathrm I}-\bm F$, is smaller for \PM\ than for \PP\ in all eleven cases tested
  (\texttt{residual\_smooth.txt}; e.g., $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=4$: $3.0\cdot10^{-5}$ versus
  $3.7\cdot10^{-4}$), whereas the \PM\ nodal error is $9$ times larger ($2.8\cdot10^{-5}$ versus
  $3.0\cdot10^{-6}$); \PH\ has the residual $3.3\cdot10^{-6}$ and the nodal error $5.1\cdot10^{-8}$. The
  ratio of discretisation to interpolation error of \PM\ is far from one where its results
  degrade (\cref{sec:interp}).
\item \emph{Stability of the unsymmetric operator.} With $\bm u_h-\bm u_{\mathrm I}=-\bm K^{-1}\bm r=
  -\sum\limits_{k}\bm v_k(\bm u_k^\top\bm r)/\sigma_k$ (singular triplets of $\bm K$;
  \texttt{spectrum\_smooth.txt}, smooth field), the error of \PP, of \PH\ (all cases) and of \PM\
  for $\alpha\le15^\circ$ is spread over the spectrum (largest single contribution $2$--$8\,\%$ of
  $\|\bm u_h-\bm u_{\mathrm I}\|^2$, from mid-spectrum modes). For $\alpha\ge30^\circ$, \PM\ has singular
  values below the smallest singular value of \PP\ (down to $0.17\,\sigma_1^{\PP}$, $\alpha=45^\circ$,
  $N=2$, $p=4$), and in four of the five cases tested a single one of the lowest six triplets
  carries $21$--$68\,\%$ of the error (the fifth, $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=4$, $p=6$: triplet $k=28$,
  $9\,\%$). The residual of the Petrov-Galerkin scheme is not orthogonal to these low modes,
  so they are excited and amplified. The lowest singular values of \PH\ agree with those of \PP\
  within $2.3\,\%$ ($k=1,\dots,50$) in all seven cases. At the upper end of the spectrum the
  difference is larger (\texttt{smin.txt}, symmetry conditions of the Lam\'e problem, $14$
  cases with $\alpha\le30^\circ$): the smallest singular values of the three global matrices
  agree to $1\,\%$, but $\sigma_{\max}(\bm K_{\PM})$ exceeds $\sigma_{\max}(\bm K_{\PP})$ by factors
  up to $20$ ($\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=4$) and $344$ ($\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=4$, $p=6$), whereas
  $\sigma_{\max}(\bm K_{\PH})/\sigma_{\max}(\bm K_{\PP})\le1.2$.
\item \emph{Ill-conditioned metric interpolation.} The Lebesgue constant
  $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}=\max_{\bx\in\Omega^e}\sum\limits_{i}|M_i^e(\bx)|$ of the trial functions (basis independent;
  sampled at the images of a $16\times16$ grid of the reference element, so the values are lower
  bounds) equals the GLL value on affine elements, but grows by orders of magnitude for \PM\
  with $\alpha$ and $p$ (\cref{tab:leb}): $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}\le130$ for $\alpha=15^\circ$, $p\le6$, but
  $1.1\cdot10^3$ ($\alpha=30^\circ$, $p=4$), $5\cdot10^5$ ($45^\circ$, $p=5$) and $1.3\cdot10^8$ ($45^\circ$,
  $p=8$). The element condition numbers of \PM\ exceed those of \PP\ by similar factors
  (\cref{tab:cond}). The images of the GLL nodes of strongly non-affine elements come close to
  configurations that are not unisolvent for $Q_p$ in skew coordinates (``interpolation
  failure'' in \citep{Rajendran2010technique}); the metric trial functions then have large
  gradients, the bilinear form loses its stability constant, and round-off is amplified
  (\cref{sec:poly}). All cases of the benign regime of \cref{sec:conv} have
  $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}\le2.4\cdot10^2$, all degraded cases $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}\ge10^3$; the converse does not hold
  strictly ($\alpha=30^\circ$, $p=6$: $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}=6.2\cdot10^3$, yet \PM\ is $620$ times more accurate than
  \PP\ at $N=8$, smooth), so $\Lambda_{\mathrm M}$ is a necessary rather than a sufficient indicator.
\item \emph{Hybrid space.} \PH\ uses metric functions only for the complete polynomials
  $P_p(\bx)$, the remaining functions are parametric. Its Lebesgue constant is the GLL value up
  to $15^\circ$ ($\le4.5$), $\le95$ at $30^\circ$ and $\le546$ at $45^\circ$ (\cref{tab:leb}), its
  element condition numbers are those of \PP\ within a factor $1.8$ for $\alpha\le30^\circ$, $p\ge3$
  (\cref{tab:cond}), and the conditioning of its interpolation matrix is much
  better than that of \PM\ at $30^\circ$, $p\ge6$ (factors $10^2$--$10^5$; $\kappa(\bm V)=2.7\cdot10^3$ against
  $2.6\cdot10^8$ at $p=7$). The large Lebesgue constants of \PM\ are therefore caused by the high-degree terms
  of $Q_p(\hat x,\hat y)$ beyond $P_p$, which are not needed for completeness. On folded
  elements (\cref{tab:leb}, $\alpha=90^\circ$: $\Lambda\le4.8\cdot10^3$) and for $p=2$ at
  $\alpha\ge30^\circ$ (\cref{tab:cond}: $23$--$117$) also \PH\ is degraded, and for some folded
  elements with $p\ge7$ its interpolation matrix is singular (\cref{sec:poly}): there the nodes
  are not unisolvent for $T_p^e$ either.
\end{enumerate}
