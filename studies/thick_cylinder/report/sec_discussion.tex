\section{Answers to the key questions}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Is distortion resistance lost for non-polynomial fields?}
Distortion \emph{immunity} in the sense of \citep{Rajendran2010technique} (results
independent of the mesh) is lost by construction: the error of a non-polynomial field
depends on the mesh, for both fields studied and for both unsymmetric elements
(\cref{tab:ratio}). For \PM, distortion \emph{resistance} depends on the distortion level and on
$p$:
\begin{itemize}
\item moderate distortion without folds ($\alpha=15^\circ$, $p\le6$; $\alpha=30^\circ$, $p\le3$):
  retained. The energy error of \PM\ exceeds the error on the undistorted mesh by
  $5$--$28\,\%$ ($15^\circ$) and $26$--$82\,\%$ ($30^\circ$, $p\le3$), whereas the nodal error of the
  conventional element is $3$--$270$ times larger ($N=8$);
\item strong distortion ($\alpha=30^\circ$ with $p\ge4$, $\alpha\ge45^\circ$) or $p\ge7$: lost. The
  \PM\ error becomes erratic and in several cases larger than that of the conventional element
  (e.g.\ $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=4$ and $8$; $\alpha=45^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=7,8$); on folded meshes its convergence
  is erratic for all $p$ (\cref{tab:rates-smooth-N}), and for $p\ge7$ at $\alpha\ge15^\circ$ it stalls at an elevated round-off
  floor.
\end{itemize}
The loss is not tied to the sign of $\det\bm J$, the quantity the formulation was
designed to be insensitive to: it already occurs on meshes with $\det\bm J>0$
($\alpha=30^\circ$) and correlates with the Lebesgue constant of the metric interpolation
(\cref{tab:leb}).

\paragraph{Are optimal convergence rates retained?}
For \PM\ in the moderate regime, yes: it converges with the optimal energy-norm order $p$ for
$p=2,\dots,6$ at $\alpha=15^\circ$ (and $p=2,3$ at $30^\circ$) for the analytic manufactured field
and for the Lam\'e field, and exponentially under $p$-refinement down to a round-off floor of
a few $10^{-10}$. The conventional element loses half of the order on the same meshes
($\eE=O(h^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor})$), because the element maps are not asymptotically affine. In the
strong regime, optimal convergence of \PM\ is lost as well: rates are erratic and the error
can grow under $h$- and $p$-refinement.

\paragraph{Practical benefit of \PM.}
Where both properties hold (moderate, non-folding distortion that persists under
refinement), the benefit over \PP\ is substantial and grows with $p$: optimal order versus
$\lfloor p/2\rfloor$, i.e.\ an error ratio $O(h^{-\lceil p/2\rceil})$ (a factor $380$ for $p=6$,
$N=8$, $\alpha=15^\circ$, smooth). It is limited in the situations for which the formulation was
proposed: on folded meshes \PM\ is not usable at high order; whether a mesh and degree fall into
the benign regime cannot be inferred from the Jacobian (non-monotone convergence already at
$\alpha=30^\circ$, $p=4$); for meshes that become asymptotically affine under refinement \PP\
recovers the optimal order, and the advantage reduces to a constant; and the unsymmetric
system requires a non-symmetric solver and a generalised Vandermonde solve per element.

\paragraph{Does the hybrid trial space remove the deficiencies?}
To a large extent, yes. With metric polynomials only for $P_p(\bx)$ and parametric functions
for the remainder, \PH\
\begin{itemize}
\item reproduces the degree-5 field to $\le1.2\cdot10^{-9}$ on all meshes, folded ones included,
  where \PM\ is limited to $10^{-4}$--$10^{-2}$ by round-off (\cref{sec:poly});
\item converges with the optimal order for all $p\le8$ on all meshes without folds, and at
  $\alpha=45^\circ$ (folded for $N\ge2$) for all $p$ (\cref{sec:conv});
\item is quasi-optimal with constant $0.78$--$1.00$ relative to its interpolation error
  (\cref{sec:interp}), and has Lebesgue constants, element condition numbers and stiffness
  spectra close to those of \PP\ (\cref{sec:mechanism});
\item is $8$--$2.6\cdot10^4$ times more accurate than \PP\ and, for $p\ge3$, $1.4$--$2.0\cdot10^5$
  times more accurate than \PM\ at $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=8$ (smooth field).
\end{itemize}
It does not remove all limits. (a) Its error constant depends on the distortion: the
amplification $e_n(\alpha)/e_n(0^\circ)$ for the Lam\'e field reaches $88$ at $\alpha=15^\circ$, $p=6$,
and where \PM\ is stable, \PH\ can be less accurate (Lam\'e, $\alpha=15^\circ$, $p\le7$: up to a
factor $3.7$), because the parametric complement approximates worse than the full $Q_p$ in skew
coordinates (\cref{sec:interp}). (b) On folded meshes its low-order results ($p=2$ at
$60^\circ$, $p\le4$ at $90^\circ$) are as unreliable as those of the other elements. (c) Its
interpolation matrix is singular for some folded elements at $p\ge7$, and its complement
requires $\det\bm J\ne0$ at the quadrature points. Within these limits \PH\ is the better choice
than \PM\ on every mesh studied, and than \PP\ on every distorted mesh studied.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
One geometry (thick cylinder), one family of distortions (self-similar rotation of
mid-side nodes), linear plane-stress elasticity, $p\le8$, $N\le8$, double precision. The
Lebesgue constants are sampled at $16\times16$ points per element and are therefore lower
bounds; they are basis independent, the round-off level is not (Lagrange basis on the
bounding box of the skew coordinates, \cref{sec:elements}). The energy norm is evaluated with
the signed $\det\bm J$; on folded meshes the nodal error is the only quantity that is defined
independently of the element mapping. The mechanism of the stability loss
(\cref{sec:mechanism}) is characterised numerically, not proven.

\section{Reproduction}\label{sec:reproduce}
The first version of this report was computed with the code base of commit \texttt{2fea864}
(monomial basis of the metric functions, no hybrid space). The present results were computed
with \texttt{MATLAB} R2026a and the code base of commit \texttt{af3e04f}, which includes the singularity checks of
\texttt{ShapeFunctions2D\_Metric} and \texttt{pSpectralElement\_NodesTopology}, the Lagrange
basis of the metric functions and the hybrid trial space (\texttt{p.metricMethod = 2}), and a
vectorised evaluation of \texttt{LagrangePolynomials} (bitwise identical results). Rejected
cases are recorded as NaN.
