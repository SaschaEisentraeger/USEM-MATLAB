\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Two unsymmetric (parametric test / metric trial) spectral elements of the unsymmetric spectral element (USEM) code
base are studied for the thick cylinder of \citet[Sec.~5.9]{Rajendran2010technique} with polynomial
degree $p=2,\dots,8$
on \texttt{Gmsh} meshes in which the internal mid-side nodes of every super-element of
$2\times2$ elements are rotated by the distortion angle $\alpha=0^\circ$--$90^\circ$ (including
folded elements), so that the distortion is kept fixed under refinement, complemented by manufactured
polynomial and analytic non-polynomial solutions on the same meshes: \PM\ (metric trial
space $Q_p$ of polynomials of degree $\le p$ in each skew coordinate) and \PH\ (hybrid trial space: complete polynomials $P_p$ in
Cartesian coordinates, complemented by parametric functions); the conventional spectral
element \PP\ serves as reference (1512 solutions, verified against the code base).
\emph{Findings.} (i) Both unsymmetric elements reproduce a degree-5 polynomial field for
$p\ge5$; for \PM\ the round-off level grows with distortion and $p$ (up to $2\cdot10^{-4}$ at
$\alpha=45^\circ$), for \PH\ it stays below $2\cdot10^{-9}$ on all meshes on which the element can be
built. (ii) For non-polynomial fields distortion immunity is lost by both. \PM\ remains
distortion tolerant and optimally convergent only for moderate distortion ($\alpha=15^\circ$,
$p\le6$; $\alpha=30^\circ$, $p\le3$); beyond, its convergence becomes erratic. \PH\ converges
with the optimal order $p$ for all $p$ on all non-folded meshes, is quasi-optimal (error
$0.78$--$1.00$ times its interpolation error) and up to $2.6\cdot10^4$ times more accurate
than \PP, which drops to order $\approx\lfloor p/2\rfloor$. (iii) The loss of \PM\ is caused by
its metric interpolation (Lebesgue constants up to $10^8$): the largest singular values of the
stiffness matrix grow up to $340$-fold relative to \PP, and single low singular modes carry
$21$--$68\,\%$ of the error; \PH\ keeps Lebesgue constants and spectra close to those of \PP. (iv) \PH\ does not remove all limits: on folded meshes its error
is still large for $p\le3$ and its interpolation matrix becomes singular for some folded
elements at $p\ge7$; for the Lam\'e field at $\alpha=15^\circ$, $p\le7$, it is up to $3.7$ times
less accurate than \PM.
\end{abstract}
