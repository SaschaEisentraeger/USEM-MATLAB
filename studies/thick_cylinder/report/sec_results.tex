\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
The complete data ($6$ angles $\times$ $4$ meshes $\times$ $7$ degrees $\times$ $3$ elements
$\times$ $3$ problems $=1512$ solutions) are in \texttt{results/raw/study\_a*.csv}.
Rates are given for the finest pair $N=4\to8$; ``--'' marks errors at the round-off
level (for \PM\ and \PH\ with $p\ge5$: less than ten times the error of the polynomial field on
the same mesh, which is the empirical round-off level, \cref{sec:poly}); ``rej.'' marks
cases in which the code base rejects the element (singular interpolation matrix,
\cref{sec:elements}); $^\dagger$ marks angles with folded elements on the meshes involved
(\cref{tab:folded}).

\subsection{Reproduction of Section 5.9 for $p=2$}\label{sec:p2}
For $p=2$ and the meshes $N=1,2,4$ of the paper, the least-squares slopes of $\eE$
(Lam\'e) are $1.90$, $1.95$, $1.14$, $1.57$ for \PM, $1.81$, $1.86$, $1.85$, $1.77$ for \PH\
and $1.68$, $1.11$, $1.07$, $0.69$ for \PP\ at $\alpha=0^\circ,30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ$
(\citet[Fig.~20]{Rajendran2010technique}: PM-QUAD8$^\ast$ $2.1$, $1.85$, $1.42$, $1.21$;
QUAD8 $1.9$, $0.95$, $0.94$, $0.98$). The trends of \PM\ and \PP\ agree with the paper: the
conventional element drops to first order on distorted meshes, the unsymmetric element stays
close to second order for $\alpha\le30^\circ$ and becomes erratic beyond. Exact agreement is not
expected, since the present $p=2$ element is the 9-node Lagrange element with $Q_2$ metric
functions in skew coordinates, not the 8-node serendipity element of the paper. \PH\ keeps a
slope of $1.77$--$1.86$ up to $\alpha=60^\circ$. On the finer pair $N=4\to8$, \PM\ and \PH\
($p=2$) converge with rate $1.94$--$1.97$ for all $\alpha\le45^\circ$, \PP\ with $1.01$--$1.07$
for $\alpha\ge15^\circ$ (\cref{tab:rates-lame-E,tab:rates-lame-N}).

\subsection{Polynomial field: distortion immunity}\label{sec:poly}
For $p\ge5$ the degree-5 field lies in both unsymmetric trial spaces on every element.
\Cref{tab:poly} shows the nodal error. \PM: on the meshes without folds
($\alpha\le30^\circ$) it is $\le7.6\cdot10^{-9}$ for all $N$, i.e.\ the field is reproduced up
to round-off, as predicted; the round-off level, however, grows with $\alpha$ and $p$ and
reaches $1.1\cdot10^{-4}$ at $\alpha=45^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=8$, and $1.6\cdot10^{-2}$ on the folded mesh
$\alpha=90^\circ$, $N=4$, $p=8$. \PH: the error is $\le5.1\cdot10^{-13}$ for $\alpha\le30^\circ$ and
$\le9.9\cdot10^{-10}$ on all meshes, folded ones included, on which the element can be built,
i.e.\ distortion immunity holds also in floating point. Both elements are rejected for the
same seven cases ($\alpha=60^\circ,90^\circ$, $p\ge6$, except \PH\ for $\alpha=60^\circ$, $N=8$, $p=6$),
in which the interpolation matrix is singular to working precision
($\mathrm{rcond}=10^{-20}$--$10^{-17}$). A dedicated test (\texttt{results/conditioning.txt})
integrates the stiffness exactly ($n_q\ge p^2+p/2$ points, \cref{sec:quad}) and evaluates the
residual $\bm K\bm u_I-\bm F$ of the exact nodal values, which vanishes in exact arithmetic. For
\PM\ it is $5\cdot10^{-14}$ at $\alpha=0^\circ$, $p=5$, but $9.9\cdot10^{-7}$ ($e_n=4\cdot10^{-5}$) at
$\alpha=60^\circ$, $N=2$, $p=8$, with $\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}(\bm K_{\PM})=1.1\cdot10^{-15}$
against $4.8\cdot10^{-8}$ for \PH\ and $2.9\cdot10^{-6}$ for \PP; for \PH\ the residual is
$\le1.2\cdot10^{-10}$ and $e_n\le8.6\cdot10^{-10}$ in all ten cases that are not rejected. For $p<5$ the polynomial
field behaves like a non-polynomial one (rates $2,3,4$ for $\alpha\le15^\circ$, \cref{fig:h-poly}).

\subsection{Non-polynomial fields: convergence}\label{sec:conv}
\Cref{fig:h-smooth,fig:h-lame} show the nodal error, \cref{fig:h-smooth-E} the energy
error for the meshes without folded elements; the rates are collected in
\cref{tab:rates-smooth-E,tab:rates-lame-E,tab:rates-smooth-N,tab:rates-lame-N}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\alpha=0^\circ$ (element maps asymptotically affine): the three elements converge
  optimally, $\eE=O(h^p)$ (smooth field: $2.00$, $2.99$, $4.00$, $4.99$, $6.00$ for
  \PM\ and \PH; Lam\'e: $1.96$--$6.69$, approaching $p$ from below as the $r^{-2}$ term is
  resolved), with almost identical errors.
\item $\alpha=15^\circ$: \PM\ keeps the optimal rates for $p\le6$ (smooth: $2.00$,
  $2.99$, $3.99$, $4.99$, $5.98$; Lam\'e: $1.97$, $2.91$, $3.90$, $4.81$, $5.83$), and its energy
  error exceeds that on the undistorted mesh by $5$--$12\,\%$ (smooth) and $5$--$28\,\%$
  (Lam\'e); for $p\ge7$ it reaches a round-off floor of about $10^{-10}$, two to three orders
  above the floor on the undistorted mesh. \PH\ is optimal for all $p$ (smooth: $2.00$, $3.00$,
  $4.00$, $4.99$, $6.01$; Lam\'e: $1.96$, $2.94$, $3.94$, $4.92$, $5.91$, $6.88$, $7.87$) and has no
  elevated floor ($\eE=6.7\cdot10^{-13}$ at $N=8$, $p=8$, smooth). \PP\ loses half of the order:
  its energy rates are $1.08$, $1.23$, $2.21$, $2.03$, $2.94$, $3.39$, $4.05$ (smooth) and, for
  the polynomial field, $0.98$, $0.95$, $1.95$, $1.94$, $2.94$, $2.95$, $3.95$, i.e.\
  $\lfloor p/2\rfloor$. This is the approximation property of the parametric space on maps that
  are not asymptotically affine: the mid-side node offset is $O(h)$, so $\bx(\bxi)$ is quadratic
  with $O(1)$ relative non-affinity, and $Q_p\circ\bx^{-1}$ contains $P_k(\bx)$ only for $2k\le p$
  (cf.\ \citep{Arnold2002Approximation,Ciarlet1972Interpolation}). At $N=8$ the \PP\ energy
  error is $5$--$2.6\cdot10^3$ times larger than that of \PH\ (smooth, $p=2,\dots,8$).
\item $\alpha=30^\circ$: \PM\ is optimal only for $p\le3$; for $p\ge4$ its convergence is erratic
  and even divergent (smooth, $p=4$: $\eE=2.2\cdot10^{-4}$ at $N=4$ and $4.3\cdot10^{-4}$ at
  $N=8$), and at $N=8$, $p=8$ it is less accurate than \PP\ ($\eE=7.3\cdot10^{-8}$ against
  $3.5\cdot10^{-9}$). \PH\ remains optimal for all $p$ (smooth: $2.00$, $3.00$, $4.01$, $5.00$,
  $6.02$; Lam\'e: $1.96$--$7.87$); at $N=8$ its energy error is $8$--$2.6\cdot10^4$ times smaller
  than that of \PP; compared with \PM\ it is equal at $p=2$ and $1.4$--$6.7\cdot10^4$ times smaller
  for $p\ge3$ (smooth). \PP\ converges with $\approx\lfloor p/2\rfloor$.
\item Folded meshes ($\alpha\ge45^\circ$; for $\alpha=45^\circ$ from $N=2$ on): \PM\ converges
  erratically in the nodal error for all $p$ (\cref{tab:rates-smooth-N}); at $\alpha=45^\circ$,
  $N=8$, $p=8$ its error is $\eE=1.6\cdot10^{-3}$. \PH\ converges at $\alpha=45^\circ$ for all
  $p$ (nodal rates $2.1$--$7.3$, smooth field: $2.55$, $3.66$, $4.75$, $5.55$; $\eE=2.5\cdot10^{-9}$ at
  $N=8$, $p=8$) and at $\alpha=60^\circ$, $90^\circ$ for $p\ge4$ and $p\ge5$, respectively. For $p=2$
  at $60^\circ$ and $p\le4$ at $90^\circ$ its error is non-monotone in $N$ and of order
  $10^{-2}$--$1$ (smooth, $\alpha=60^\circ$, $p=2$: $e_n=0.34$ at $N=8$; Lam\'e, $\alpha=90^\circ$, $p=4$:
  $e_n=3.9$ at $N=2$), as are the errors of the other two elements. \PP\
  converges monotonically at the reduced rate for $\alpha=45^\circ$; on the folded meshes
  $\alpha\ge60^\circ$ its convergence is irregular for $p\le3$, but its nodal error still
  decreases by $1.5$--$3$ orders of magnitude between $N=1$ and $N=8$ for $p\ge4$
  (\cref{fig:h-smooth}).
\end{enumerate}
Under $p$-refinement on a fixed mesh (\cref{fig:p}), \PM\ converges exponentially for
$\alpha\le15^\circ$ down to its round-off floor (reached at $p\approx6$ for $N=8$), like \PP\ on the
undistorted mesh; at $\alpha=30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ its error is non-monotone in $p$ (smooth
field, $N=8$, $\alpha=30^\circ$: $e_n$ jumps from $2.4\cdot10^{-6}$ at $p=3$ to $2.8\cdot10^{-5}$ at
$p=4$ and grows from $5.2\cdot10^{-11}$ at $p=6$ to $4.1\cdot10^{-9}$ at $p=8$). \PH\ converges
exponentially down to $3.5\cdot10^{-13}$ ($\alpha=30^\circ$, $p=7$) and $1.1\cdot10^{-11}$
($\alpha=45^\circ$, $p=7$), \PP\ monotonically, but more slowly than on the undistorted mesh.

\subsection{Approximation versus stability}\label{sec:interp}
The exact nodal values inserted into the trial functions of each element give the
interpolation error of its trial space (\texttt{run\_interpolation\_study.m},
\texttt{results/raw/interp\_a*.csv}); the ratio of discretisation to interpolation error
separates the stability of the discrete problem from the approximation property of the
space (\cref{tab:interp}). For \PH\ the ratio is $0.78$--$1.00$ in all $158$ cases on the meshes
without folds in which the interpolation error is above the round-off level (all $N$, $p$, both
non-polynomial fields), for \PP\ $0.84$--$1.00$: both are quasi-optimal with a constant close to
one. For \PM\ it is $0.98$--$1.01$ at $\alpha=15^\circ$, $p\le6$, but ranges from $0.07$ to $10^5$
overall ($0.2$--$64$ at $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=8$); the degraded \PM\ results are therefore not
explained by the approximation property of its space. The interpolation errors of the two
unsymmetric spaces differ as well. For the Lam\'e field at $\alpha=15^\circ$ the \PH\ space
approximates worse (interpolation error $1.15$--$5.7$ times that of \PM, $N=8$), which accounts for
the larger \PH\ errors there (up to a factor $4.1$ in $\eE$ for $p\le7$); for the smooth field
at $\alpha=30^\circ$ the \PM\ interpolant is up to $9$ times less accurate for $p\le6$
because of its large Lebesgue constants (\cref{sec:mechanism}).

\subsection{Distortion sensitivity}\label{sec:sens}
\Cref{tab:ratio} gives the error amplification $e_n(\alpha)/e_n(0^\circ)$ on the
finest mesh, \cref{fig:alpha} the nodal error versus $\alpha$. For a non-polynomial
field none of the elements is distortion immune: the errors depend on $\alpha$. For
$\alpha=15^\circ$ and $p\le6$ the amplification is $1.5$--$2.6$ (smooth) and $3.2$--$4.7$ (Lam\'e)
for \PM, $1.6$--$2.4$ and $6.4$--$93$ for \PH, against $4.8$--$99$ and $61$--$2900$ for \PP. At
$30^\circ$ the \PM\ amplification reaches $2.2\cdot10^3$ (smooth, $p=4$) and $10^4$ (Lam\'e, $p=8$),
whereas that of \PH\ stays $\le4.7$ (smooth) and $\le381$ (Lam\'e), that of \PP\ reaches
$3.4\cdot10^3$ and $3.6\cdot10^4$. On the folded meshes the \PM\ amplification exceeds the \PP\
amplification by up to six orders of magnitude (Lam\'e, $45^\circ$, $p=8$); that of \PH\ is smaller than that of
\PP\ for all $p$ at $45^\circ$ and for all $p\ge3$ at $60^\circ$ (rejected cases excepted). (For $p\ge7$ the reference errors at $\alpha=0^\circ$
are at round-off level, so these columns underestimate the sensitivity.) The amplification
of \PH\ for the Lam\'e field grows with $p$ ($7.1$ at $p=2$, $93$ at $p=6$, $\alpha=15^\circ$): its
error constant depends on the distortion, its rate does not (\cref{tab:rates-lame-E}).

\subsection{Quadrature}\label{sec:quad}
The integrand \eqref{eq:Kpm} of \PM\ is a polynomial of degree $(2p-1)(q+1)$ per direction
for a geometry of degree $q$; exact integration needs $n_q\ge p^2+p/2$ points on the
elements with exact arcs ($q=p$) and $n_q\ge3p-1$ on the interior elements ($q=2$). The
complement of the \PH\ trial functions involves $\bm J^{-1}$, so its integrand is rational and is
not integrated exactly by any rule. The default rule ($n_q=2p$) is therefore not exact.
Repeating selected cases with $n_q=2p,3p,4p,6p$ (\texttt{results/raw/quadrature.csv};
$\alpha=0^\circ,30^\circ$, $N=1,2$, $p=5,8$) changes $\eE$ of the non-polynomial fields by at most
$3\cdot10^{-7}$ (relative) for \PP, $5\cdot10^{-6}$ for \PH\ and $0.5\,\%$ for \PM; the single exception
(\PM, smooth field, $\alpha=30^\circ$, $N=2$, $p=8$: $\eE$ varies by a factor $2.7$) has an error of
the order of the round-off level of that mesh (polynomial field with the same rules:
$6\cdot10^{-9}$--$7\cdot10^{-8}$). For the polynomial field ($p\ge5$), whose error is pure
round-off, the value changes by up to a factor $21$ for \PM\ and $2.7$ for \PH\ (at
$\le10^{-13}$). The conclusions are therefore not affected by quadrature. On folded meshes the
\PM\ results change erratically with $n_q$ (e.g.\ Lam\'e, $\alpha=90^\circ$, $N=1$, $p=5$:
$\eE=38$ for $n_q=10$, $0.66$ for $n_q\ge15$), consistent with the round-off amplification of
\cref{sec:poly}.
